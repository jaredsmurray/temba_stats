A cross-tabulation records combinations

A cross-tabulation counts observations in every combination of two categories.

Purpose Repaid Default Total
Debt consolidation 41,278 8,131 49,409
Home improvement 5,139 717 5,856
Small business 2,369 822 3,191
Total 48,786 9,670 58,456

The interior cells describe pairs of outcomes. The margins summarize each variable on its own.

Joint and marginal proportions

Both use the full sample as their denominator.

Question Calculation Proportion
Small business and default 822 / 58,456 0.0141
Small business, either outcome 3,191 / 58,456 0.0546

A joint proportion describes a combination. A marginal proportion describes one category, summing over the other variable.

From observed proportions to a probability model

We can use historical proportions as estimated probabilities for a future loan if the historical loans represent that future population.

Purpose Repaid (D = 0) Default (D = 1)
Debt consolidation (P = 1) 0.706 0.139
Home improvement (P = 2) 0.088 0.012
Small business (P = 3) 0.041 0.014

The six interior probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one. The numbers now describe possible outcomes for a future loan.

Conditioning a probability table

\[ \Pr(A\mid B)=\frac{\Pr(A\text{ and }B)}{\Pr(B)},\qquad \Pr(B)>0. \]

Keep the outcomes consistent with the condition, then divide by their total probability.

For the loan model, dividing the small-business/default joint probability by the small-business margin gives the same rate as dividing their counts.
